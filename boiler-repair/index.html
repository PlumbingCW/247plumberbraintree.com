No heat and the temperature is dropping

A boiler that has stopped heating on a freezing night puts your pipes at risk as well as your comfort. Blue Hill, the nearest federal weather record, averages about 121 days a year with lows at or below freezing, and 48 inches is the design frost depth in the Massachusetts residential code. Pipes in exterior walls, garages, and crawlspaces are the first to suffer.

While you wait, open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls, let a faucet drip, and keep interior doors open so heat spreads. Do not use an oven or a camp stove for heat. If a pipe has already frozen, shut the main valve and see our burst pipe repair page.

  • Check that the boiler switch, thermostat, and breaker are on.
  • Look at the pressure gauge and note the reading.
  • Do not open the boiler cabinet or the burner.
  • Never bypass a safety cutout or a relief valve.

What boiler repair covers, and what it does not

Plumbing and gas fitting are regulated by the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters, and we work within that scope. That covers piping, valves, circulators, diverters, air vents, expansion tanks, relief valves, fill valves and leaks. Oil burner work is separate. The Department of Fire Services issues its own oil burner technician certificate through its Boiler and Pressure Vessel program, covering service, installation, and repair of oil burners.

That means if your fault is in an oil burner, the nozzle, the primary control, or the fuel unit, the right person holds a different certificate. We tell you early which side the fault is on, so you do not pay for a visit that cannot fix it. Ask any company who will handle each part before work starts.

Hydronic and steam systems fail in different ways

Older homes often have one of two systems. Knowing which yours is helps both of us.

Hot water baseboard.

Water circulates through copper pipe and finned tube. Air pockets, a dead circulator, and stuck zone valves are the usual plumbing faults.

Cast iron radiators on hot water.

Heavy radiators with air vents at the top. A radiator that stays cold at the top usually needs bleeding or has a failed vent.

One pipe steam.

Steam rises to the radiator through a single pipe and the condensate returns through the same one. Slope, vent valves, and water level are everything.

Two pipe steam.

A separate return carries condensate back. Failed traps and clogged returns cause banging and uneven heat.

Low water cutoff and sight glass.

On steam boilers, the water level in the sight glass is the first thing we read. A low reading shuts the boiler off, and a blow down of the cutoff keeps it reliable.

Indirect water heater loop.

Many boilers also heat an indirect tank. A failed diverter or loop circulator takes the hot water down with the heat.

How we trace a boiler with no heat

We work from outside the boiler in, so we do not take apart a working unit.

1

Read the system.

Gauge pressure and temperature, the sight glass on steam, and any fault light tell us the state before we touch a valve.

2

Check flow and zones.

We feel pipes at the circulator and zone valves, bleed air from the lines, and confirm each zone calls for heat and gets it.

3

Test the water side parts.

Expansion tank charge, fill valve, relief valve, diverter, and air vents are tested or replaced as needed.

4

Hand off or finish.

If the fault is on the burner side, we explain who to call. If the fault is ours, we repair it, restart the system, and confirm every zone heats.

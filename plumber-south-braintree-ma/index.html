South Braintree, Braintree, MA

South Braintree grew up around the Monatiquot River mills and the Old Colony railroad junction, and it is still a mix of homes and businesses. If you need a plumber in South Braintree, MA, call (617) 752-0573, say whether it is a house, an apartment or a business, and ask when someone can come out.

The Massachusetts Historical Commission's 1979 survey describes South Braintree as an early industrial hub, with Greek Revival, Italianate and Victorian housing packed along Washington, Tremont, Hancock and Franklin Streets. A real estate data site lists detached houses alongside two-to-four-unit and larger buildings. Those are secondary figures.

The takeaway: a South Braintree call can involve a single-family house, a tenant in a multi-unit building, or a small commercial space. Who pays or who authorizes the work changes with each.

Older buildings, repairs done in stages

Buildings that date to the Greek Revival and Victorian eras have usually been repiped, relined or patched a section at a time. We do not assume any particular material in any South Braintree address. A plumber needs to look at the exposed pipe and the age of each section before recommending a repair.

What it means for you: if a leak keeps returning at different points, ask whether repiping makes more sense than another patch. If you are in a multi-unit building, a camera look inside the main drain can show whether a shared line is the real source.

Common South Braintree calls

Dense streets and older buildings produce a predictable set of questions.

Kitchen and bath drains that keep slowing.

Repeat clogs are a sign to look farther down the line. See drain cleaning and hydro jetting for heavier buildup.

Leaks that return after a patch.

A repair that fails twice points to the surrounding pipe, not the joint. Ask about the condition of the nearby sections.

Restaurant and shop plumbing.

Braintree's rules put grease traps under the Board of Health with approval from the Inspector of Plumbing and Gas. See commercial plumbing.

Backflow and cross-connection checks.

The Town runs a cross-connection program, and new backflow preventers need design approval and a Massachusetts-licensed plumber.

The 2010 flood and Hancock Street

The Town's own page on the March 2010 flooding names the Hancock and Washington Street intersection among the locations affected when the Monatiquot overflowed. That is a historical fact about the intersection, not a prediction for any building. If you own near that area, check that floor drains work, that any sump discharges outdoors and that stored items sit off the floor.

Questions to ask before work starts

These apply to any plumber you hire in South Braintree.

  • Will a permit be pulled, and by whom.
  • What is the price and what does it include.
  • Can you check the plumber's license on the state lookup.
  • Will the plumber photograph the problem before and after.
  • What happens if the cause turns out to be a shared line.

Codes and sources behind this page

The governing standard for plumbing work in Massachusetts is the Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, and permits are governed by 248 CMR 3.00. Licenses come from the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters, and the state offers an occupational license lookup so you can confirm any plumber before work starts.

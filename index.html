Braintree homes

Why plumbing problems here follow the age of the house

Braintree has about 15,000 housing units, and the Census Bureau puts the median year a home was built at 1957. Roughly three out of four homes in town predate 1980. That matters because older houses are more likely to have galvanized or older copper supply lines, cast iron drains and original water heaters at the end of their life.

If your home is in that group, treat a rusty tap, a damp basement ceiling or a drain that backs up twice in a month as early warnings. A midnight pipe failure usually has weeks of signs that came first, which is why a leak detection visit or a camera look at the sewer line can be cheaper than waiting.

About 78 percent of occupied homes in town are owner occupied, so most plumbing decisions belong to the people living there. That also means the part of the pipe you own is your responsibility, which brings us to the water and sewer rules below.

Water and sewer

Where your responsibility starts and the Town's ends

Braintree runs its own Water and Sewer Department, and drinking water comes from the Great Pond reservoir system. Under the Town's rules the homeowner keeps the service pipe from the curb stop to the house in working order. If a leak is found on that stretch, the owner has a short window to hire a contractor before the Town can step in.

Sewer laterals work the same way in spirit. The pipe from your house to the property line is generally the owner's to maintain, and the section under the street is the Town's. The Town's own wording on this is not perfectly aligned, so confirm the boundary with the Water and Sewer Department before assuming who pays for a sewer line repair.

One more rule worth knowing: sump pumps, roof drains and foundation drains may not discharge into the sanitary sewer. A basement pump has to send water outside, and a sump pump installation should be planned with that in mind.

Cold weather

Freeze season and after-hours plumbing calls

Massachusetts building code uses a 48 inch frost line, and the nearest long running weather station, at Blue Hill in Milton, records low temperatures at or below freezing on roughly 120 days a year. Pipes in exterior walls, unheated garages and crawl spaces are the first to freeze.

For a homeowner, that means a few practical habits. Know where the main shutoff is before winter, disconnect outdoor hoses early and open the cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during a deep freeze. If a pipe does freeze or split, our burst pipe repair plumbers work nights and weekends, and the same line handles a failed boiler through boiler repair.

Before we arrive

Five things to do while you wait for the plumber

These steps limit damage and keep the work area safe. None of them require tools.

  • Shut off the main water valve, usually near the meter or where the pipe enters the basement.
  • Turn off power to wet rooms at the breaker panel if it is safe to reach.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from the water.
  • Take a few photos for your records before cleanup.
  • Keep the phone nearby and the area clear for the plumber.

Codes and sources behind this page

The governing standard for plumbing work in Massachusetts is the Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, and permits are governed by 248 CMR 3.00. Licenses come from the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters, and the state offers an occupational license lookup so you can confirm any plumber before work starts.

Wollaston and the older shared buildings

Quincy's plan for the 50.75-acre Wollaston Urban Revitalization District says about 66 percent of its buildings predate 1940. Buildings that old carry the oldest drain stacks in the city. If a kitchen sink backs up in one unit, we check whether a shared stack is the cause before we cable only that fixture, using drain cleaning at the right point in the system.

About 62 percent of Quincy's housing units sit in buildings with two or more units, and roughly 11 percent are in buildings of three or four units. In a shared building, a backup on the lowest floor often starts higher up. Before we run a cable, we ask which units are affected, because that tells us whether to work at a fixture or on the building's main drain with sewer line cleaning.

Single-family house or multi-unit building: what changes in an emergency

The same leak calls for different first moves depending on the building.

Single-family home.

  • One main shutoff controls everything, usually in the basement.
  • You decide quickly and approve the scope yourself.
  • A backup usually points to your own line.
  • Check the water heater closet and the basement floor drain first.

Two to four units and larger buildings.

  • A valve may control one unit or the whole building, so find out which.
  • Call the owner or manager before opening a ceiling.
  • A backup on the lowest floor can start in a stack serving several units.
  • Ask neighbors below to check ceilings for stains or drips.

Water from the MWRA, and what it means at your fixtures

The City of Quincy buys its drinking water from the MWRA through five master meters, and the city distributes it. The city's 2025 report estimates that about 20 percent of its pipes are unlined cast iron more than 85 years old. It replaced 6.5 miles of main that year. If rust-colored water appears after street work, run the cold tap until it clears before blaming your own pipes.

The MWRA disinfects with monochloramine, and its 2025 average was 1.99 ppm. Rubber flappers and seals wear out in every water system, so we do not blame the water for a worn part without looking at it. If a flapper or supply-line gasket fails again and again, ask us to inspect the part and the fill valve rather than replacing it a third time.

Quincy has set a goal of removing identified lead service lines by 2034. If your home is on that list, tell us before we start work near the street side of your water line, so we can coordinate rather than cut into something the city plans to replace.

These steps limit damage while an emergency visit is arranged.

Sewer backups, the city system and your line

Quincy entered a Consent Decree with the US Department of Justice in 2021 over its sewer system, and the city publishes sewer overflow reports and a map. Those reports define a sewer backup and ask residents to keep fats, oils and grease out of drains. The practical point for you is that if several fixtures back up at once, the blockage may be in your lateral or in the city main.

A sewer camera inspection tells us which. It also gives you a recording to show the city's Water, Sewer and Drain Division if the problem sits on their side. We have not confirmed Quincy's rule on who owns each section of lateral, so ask the division before assuming who pays.

Permits and the Quincy inspectors

Quincy's Inspectional Services Department runs its Plumbing and Gas Department from the Public Works Building at 55 Sea Street, and its main line is (617) 376-1450. Under state rule 248 CMR 3.05, plumbing or gas work may not be replaced or repaired until a permit is issued, and permits go only to licensed individuals. That is another reason to hire a licensed plumber rather than a handyman.

The rule also allows a licensee to start an emergency repair first, if the work is tested and safe and the inspector is notified, with inspection no later than the next working day. In practice, a burst pipe at midnight does not wait for the office to open.

Emergency Commercial Plumbing in Braintree, MA

When a business loses water, or a floor drain starts backing up during service, the clock runs against you, and our 24/7 line in Braintree, MA is meant for that moment. While you wait, close the main shutoff if water is pouring, stop using the affected fixtures and clear equipment and stock off the floor.

Commercial plumbing covers the pipes and fixtures in non-residential buildings: restaurants, retail, offices, small industrial space and multi-unit rental properties. The pipes are larger, the loads heavier and the rules stricter than in a house. We handle urgent repairs at night and on weekends, and we also schedule the planned work that prevents them.

Water or sewage on the floor of your business

A commercial leak or backup affects customers, staff and stock. Move quickly on the steps that limit damage.

  • Shut the main water valve if a pipe has burst or a fixture is pouring.
  • Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel dry.
  • Stop using sinks, toilets and floor drains that are backing up.

Restaurants and kitchens: grease is the daily enemy

Kitchens put fat, starch and food solids into the drain all day. Left alone, that mix hardens into a lining that narrows the pipe, which is why a restaurant line can back up at the worst time, in the middle of a dinner rush. Regular jetting and keeping the trap serviced prevent most of these calls. The method is explained on our hydro jetting page.

Town rules matter here. Grease traps and interceptors fall under the Board of Health, and the Town Inspector of Plumbing and Gas inspects and approves them. Restaurants and similar businesses must install a suitable trap that the inspector approves, so ask the inspector before changing or relocating one.

Retail and shopping centers

South Shore Plaza sits at 250 Granite Street in Braintree, and the area has plenty of retail and food space. We are not describing any customer there, only the geography. Stores and food counters in shopping buildings often share drain and water lines, so a fault in one unit can reach the next.

That means a good first step in any retail callout is learning which units share the affected line. It saves time, and it helps your landlord or property manager route the repair and the cost properly.

Landlords and property managers

Multi-unit and mixed-use properties produce calls from tenants at all hours. We can coordinate with a manager, work from a unit list and provide notes on what we found at each address. Tell us who has authority to approve work, so that a night repair is not delayed.

Recurring problems in a building often point to a shared stack or main. For the sewer side, see sewer line cleaning, and for hidden water loss, see leak detection.

Backflow testing and cross-connection control

Backflow prevention is planned work, not an emergency. Braintree has a Cross-Connection Control By-Law, and the Water and Sewer Department runs surveys, registration and a testing program for backflow preventers. Commercial, industrial and institutional owners must have their plumbing surveyed by the water purveyor.

A new backflow preventer needs design approval from the Department, must be installed by a Massachusetts licensed plumber and must be approved on completion. The Town materials we read do not state who performs routine tests or how often, so ask the Department what applies to your device.

Commercial leak detection

Many business owners call about a water bill that rose without explanation. Hidden leaks in slabs, walls and buried lines are traceable with pressure tests and listening gear, and we do that work. Be aware that a leak found on a rented space may be the landlord's to repair, so check your lease.

We will tell you honestly if a trace is likely to be long or inconclusive. For the full method list, see leak detection.

Storm drains and parking lot water

Water that pools in a lot or loading area is usually a storm drain problem, not a sanitary one. Local rules prohibit sending roof and area drainage into the sanitary sewer. If a catch basin is full of sediment or debris, we can clear it, but a drainage design problem needs the property owner and an engineer.

What shapes a commercial plumbing price

Commercial jobs vary a great deal in scale. We keep the pricing conversation clear.

Building size and access. Large pipe, roof access and tight ceiling spaces change the time required.
Hours of the call. Overnight and weekend crews are smaller. Same-day visits when available and after-hours visits are priced for that, and we say so first.
Equipment needed. Jetting, camera work and lifts add to a job.
Parts and fixtures. Commercial grade valves and fixtures differ in cost and lead time.
Permits and approvals. Some work needs inspector sign-off or Water and Sewer Department approval, which affects timing.

Codes and sources behind this page

The governing standard for plumbing work in Massachusetts is the Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, and permits are governed by 248 CMR 3.00. Licenses come from the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters, and the state offers an occupational license lookup so you can confirm any plumber before work starts.

Water where it should not be: leaks and wet lower levels

A ceiling stain, a damp wall or a sudden jump in the water bill usually means a leak you cannot see. The quickest test is to shut off every fixture and watch the water meter. If it keeps turning, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and the fixtures. A leak detection visit uses pressure tests and listening gear to find the spot before anyone cuts a wall.

If water is actively pouring, close the main valve first and call us second. Do not wait for the stain to grow. A burst supply line can release gallons a minute, and a fast shutoff saves flooring and drywall.

Drains that back up or run slow

One slow sink is usually a local clog, and a cable or a hand auger clears it. When the tub, the toilet and the floor drain all slow down together, the blockage is further down, in the main line. For a single fixture, drain cleaning is the right step. For a main line, sewer line cleaning with a camera look reaches the roots or scale that a fixture cable cannot.

Holbrook's sewage goes to the MWRA system, which lists the town for sewer service only. Holbrook Public Works is at 50 North Franklin Street. We have not found a published Holbrook rule on sump pump discharge, so ask the town before connecting any discharge to a sewer line.

No hot water or a leaking tank

If the tank is leaking from the base, the steel is breached and the unit is finished. Shut off the water to the heater, turn the thermostat or gas control to off, and call us. A tank that only runs cold may need a thermostat, an element or an igniter, which are repairs. Replacing a tank is a choice between water heater repair and a new unit.

A water heater installed above living space needs a safe waste pan under the code, and a replacement may also need a plumbing permit. Holbrook's Building Department is at 50 North Franklin Street, floor 2R, reached from the front parking lot. The number is 781-767-9062, open Monday, Wednesday and Thursday 7:30 to 4:30, Tuesday 7:30 to 6:30, and closed Friday.

Where Holbrook's water comes from, and what it means at the tap

Holbrook buys all of its water from the Randolph-Holbrook Joint Water System through an interconnection off Water Street. The town bought 225,203,714 gallons in 2025, and the water is produced at the Great Pond Water Treatment Plant in Randolph. The report lists hardness at 56 mg/L from one sample, about 3.3 grains per gallon by arithmetic.

That is on the soft side, so scale in a water heater builds slowly. The report also says chlorine was temporarily added to Holbrook's storage tanks in summer 2025 to raise residuals. If you notice a change in taste or odor, call Holbrook Public Works. A brief change is not a plumbing fault inside your home.

Lake Holbrook and the Cochato River appear in the town's report as waters listed as impaired by EPA for bacteria and phosphorus. Your tap water does not come from them, since it comes from the Great Pond system. They matter for low-lying yards, where a sump pump should discharge outdoors and away from the foundation.

A small town with a long history

Holbrook was set apart from Randolph on February 29, 1872, and was once known as East Randolph, a leading boot and shoe center. Most of today's housing is later than that history, with a 1957 median build year. The old name is a reminder that Holbrook and Randolph share roots and, today, a water supply.

If you are not sure which town an address falls in, tell us the street. We also serve Weymouth, which borders Holbrook on the east, and neighboring ZIP codes 02184, 02368 and 02188 through 02191 get the same dispatch line.

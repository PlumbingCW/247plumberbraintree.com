Boiler Diverter Valve Repair in Braintree, MA

A diverter valve decides where your boiler's hot water goes. Here is how to tell which kind you have, what failure looks like, and what is safe to check yourself.

By the Braintree 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 30, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026.

A boiler diverter valve sends hot water to one place or another. On a combination boiler it switches heat between your radiators and your hot taps. On an older one pipe hot water system, a diverter tee pushes part of the flow into each radiator. When either fails, the symptoms are heat but no hot water, hot water but no heat, or uneven radiators.

If your boiler is working in Braintree, MA but one half of the job is missing, the diverter is a suspect. The sections below help you tell which type you have, what a repair usually involves, and which checks are safe before you call.

Two different parts share the name

Homeowners call both of these a diverter valve, but they fail in different ways and are fixed in different ways.

Three way diverter valve (combi boiler)

  • Sits inside or beside a combination boiler.
  • A motor head moves a valve to send heat to radiators or to the hot tap.
  • Failure shows as heat with no hot water, or the reverse.
  • Often repaired by replacing the cartridge or motor head, not the whole boiler.

Diverter tee (one pipe hydronic loop)

  • A fixed fitting on the main loop with no moving parts.
  • Uses flow direction to pull some water into each radiator.
  • Failure shows as one radiator cold while others run hot.
  • Often a placement, air or flow problem, not a broken part.

Signs the diverter is the problem

Look for a pattern before you assume the valve. Several other parts can mimic it.

Heat but no hot water.

On a combi boiler, the valve may be stuck in the heating position so water never reaches the hot tap.

Hot water but no heat.

The valve is stuck on the hot water side, or the motor head is not moving it back after a tap is closed.

Both at once, weakly.

A worn seal or a half-open valve can send heat to both circuits and leave neither at full strength.

Humming, banging or clicking.

A motor head that is trying to turn a seized valve can click or hum without moving it.

Radiator cold at one end of a loop.

On a one pipe system, a diverter tee installed backwards or choked with sludge starves the radiators beyond it.

Water around the valve.

A weeping seal on the valve body can drip onto the boiler. It needs prompt repair before it reaches electrical parts.

A pump that has failed, trapped air and sludge can look the same, so a plumber tests the pump and flow before replacing parts.

What you can check, and what to leave alone

Safe checks are limited to what is visible and does not involve opening sealed parts.

CheckSafe for a homeowner?Why.
Boiler pressure gauge.Yes.Low pressure points to a leak or a need for a refill by the maker's method.
Radiator thermostats and room thermostat setting.Yes.A wrong setting can mimic a failed valve.
Bleeding a radiator with a key.Yes.Releases trapped air, but catch the drip and close it as soon as water appears.
Listening for the valve motor click.Yes.Silence when you open a tap suggests the motor head or wiring.
Removing the valve cartridge.No.The system must be drained or isolated and pressure released first.

How a plumber repairs a diverter valve

1

Confirm which valve it is.

The plumber identifies the boiler type, finds the valve and tests whether the motor head moves it before blaming the valve body.

2

Isolate and relieve pressure.

Isolation valves are closed and pressure is bled off. On sealed systems, part of the water is drained to avoid a spill.

3

Replace the failed part.

A worn cartridge, seal or motor head is swapped. If the valve body is cracked or corroded, the whole valve is replaced.

4

Refill, bleed and test.

The system is refilled to the correct pressure, air is bled out, and both heating and hot water are run to prove the valve switches cleanly.

Repair or replace the boiler

A diverter is a modest part on a sound boiler, and replacing it is usually far cheaper than a new boiler. The calculation changes if the heat exchanger is leaking, the boiler is very old, or parts are no longer made. Ask the plumber for the boiler's age, its repair history and what else is near the end of life.

If the answer points toward replacement, you may be choosing between a combi unit and a boiler with a separate hot water tank.

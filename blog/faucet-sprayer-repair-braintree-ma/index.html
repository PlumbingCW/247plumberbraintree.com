Faucet Sprayer Repair in Braintree, MA: Why It Went Weak, Leaks or Sticks

Most sprayer problems come from a clogged nozzle, a kinked hose or a diverter that has stopped switching. Each has a quick check.

A kitchen faucet sprayer in Braintree, MA that has gone weak, leaks or will not switch is usually suffering from one of three things. These are a clogged nozzle face, a kinked or blocked hose, or a diverter that no longer sends water to the spray. Each has a check you can do in a few minutes with a bowl of vinegar water and a flashlight.

Leaks are different. A drip from the head or under the sink points to a worn hose, washer or connection, and those parts are replaceable. This page walks through the checks in order so you do not replace parts that were never broken.

Start with what you see and hear, then follow the matching row.

Check a weak sprayer in this order

Do these in order. The first two are free and fix a lot of cases.

1

Soak the nozzle.

Unscrew the sprayer face or aerator, drop it in warm vinegar water for a while and pick out the holes with a pin. Rinse and reattach.

2

Check the hose path.

Open the cabinet and follow the hose from the faucet to the connections. Look for kinks, a loop that has fallen below its weight or something pressed against it.

3

Test with the sprayer removed.

Disconnect the hose at the supply, point it in a bucket and briefly open the water. If flow is strong there, the hose and faucet are fine and the sprayer head is the problem.

4

Inspect the diverter.

Pull the spout or the cap and check the small valve that switches flow. Debris or a worn seal can leave it half open, which weakens the spray.

How a kitchen sprayer sends water where it should

When you press the sprayer, a small internal valve called a diverter blocks the spout and sends water down the hose. In a separate side spray, the diverter sits in the faucet body. In a pull-down or pull-out faucet, the hose is the spout and the diverter is part of the sprayer head.

Knowing which design you have saves time. A side spray that stops working often has a bad diverter or a worn hose, while a pull-down that sprays sideways almost always has a clogged head.

Common sprayer faults and what repairs them

Most parts are standard and inexpensive. The trick is to match the part to your faucet brand and model.

Clogged spray head.

Debris or deposits block the holes. Soaking and picking them clear usually restores the pattern.

Worn diverter.

The valve no longer seals fully, so spray is weak and the spout drips. The diverter is a replaceable part for most models.

Kinked or cracked hose.

A hose that has been squeezed or has aged develops a weak spot. Replacement hoses come in standard lengths.

Loose or worn connection.

Fittings under the sink loosen over time and the washers wear. A leak here shows as drips on the cabinet floor.

Slipped counterweight.

On pull-down models a weight on the hose keeps it retracting. If it slides up, the hose sticks out or tangles.

Worn faucet cartridge.

If the faucet itself is dripping or the handle is loose, the cartridge may be the cause and the sprayer a side effect.

Fix the sprayer or replace the faucet

Sprayer parts are cheap, but not every faucet is worth keeping.

Repair is a sound choice when.

  • The faucet is otherwise in good condition.
  • Parts for your brand and model are available.
  • The fault is a head, hose or diverter.
  • You like the faucet's reach and finish.

Replacement is a better choice when.

  • Several parts are worn and the faucet is old.
  • The finish is pitted or the body leaks at the base.
  • Parts for the model can no longer be found.
  • You were already planning a sink or counter update.

When a repair is bigger than the sprayer

A sprayer that fails again and again, a faucet that leaks at the base and low pressure at every tap are signs the problem is not the sprayer. Corroded supply lines, a failing shutoff or a plumbing issue behind the wall will need a plumber's eye. Our faucet repair page covers the cartridge, stem and valve work that goes with it.

If you decide to replace the faucet, a plumbing installation visit covers new supply lines and shutoffs at the same time, which is cheaper than doing them in separate trips.

What Braintree's soft water means for a clogged sprayer

The Braintree Water and Sewer Department's 2024 Water Quality Report lists hardness at 47.8 mg/L. That is about 2.8 grains per gallon by an approximate conversion, which is soft water. Mineral scale is a smaller cause of clogged nozzles here than in hard water towns, so if your sprayer clogs repeatedly, look for sediment or grit from the pipes rather than assuming scale.

The same report puts pH at 7.75, which is in the normal range.
